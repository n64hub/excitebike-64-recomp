# Excitebike 64's frame-dump plan: one run of the desktop build through every screen a player meets, played in a 16:9,
# a 4:3 and a 32:9 window while the build dumps its frames, the three dumps then checked against each other by machine.
# A check fails only what a plan plays, so a scene a player reports a fault in that this doesn't reach is added here by
# the work that fixes it.
# Each line is a step at seconds since the program started: `key <name>` presses and releases a key in the window,
# `down <name>` and `up <name>` hold and release one, `shot <name>` captures the window, `poke <addr> <value>` writes a
# 32-bit word of RDRAM, and `until <addr> <mask> <value> <timeout s> [key]` waits until the word at addr anded with
# mask is value, pressing key every 1.5 s meanwhile if it isn't yet; the later steps move on by its wait, so the three
# runs keep step with the game, not the clock, and an until unmet by its timeout fails the run. Addresses are the USA
# Rev 1 ROM's (syms/). Keys are the keyboard's defaults: Enter Start, Space A, Shift B, the arrows the stick.
# The screen shown is the word at 0x800F5164, a Screen of the table at 0x800CDF7C, 0x2C bytes each: the main menu
# 0x800CE0DC, Options 0x800CE058, Special Tracks 0x800CE0B0, Rider Select 0x800CE108, the title 0x800CE1E4. The game
# state is the word at 0x800C3308: 0x800C32B4 a race, 0x800C3298 the menus. gGame is at 0x800F0710: its main menu
# cursor at +4 (0x800F0714, the modes from 0 in the menu's order), Options' cursor in the high half of +0x448
# (0x800F0B58). The pause menu is the word at 0x800E40B0 (1 its items, 4 Really Quit or Just Kidding), its cursor at
# 0x800E4108 (3 Quit, then 0 Really Quit), and a race's phase at 0x800E4098 is 1 once its track's intro takes A and 6
# once a quit is taken and it fades out; gGame's flags (0x800F0710) keep 0x80 set until the intro is over. A menu fades
# in under its screen word and drops a key in the fade, and an intro its first frames, so each step whose miss would
# leave the plan out of step presses until RAM shows it took. A race is held to the throttle from the moment it is under
# way (the event's state at 0x800F0F48 is 5), with no steering, yet the three runs still race apart within seconds:
# bike_update steps each rider by the retraces the last frame took, which differ with each window's drawing, so a
# race's middle at 16:9 or 32:9 matches no frame of the 4:3 run's race. So each race, from there to the menu after it
# (its pause menu, results and replay over it), is left out of the middle (`unchecked middle` to `checked middle`),
# the credits' race too. The span starts as the race loads: its track's intro, and the rider screen before Time
# Trials and the special tracks, follow a rider round the course by the same bike_update and ride apart within seconds
# too (the first race's intro a view 1.6 s in a row at 16:9 that the 4:3 run never showed). And the title is left
# out of where the HUD sits (`unchecked hud`): its logo stays in the middle at 4:3 in every window over its attract
# race, which widens, as a HUD left at 4:3 would.
# The track editor is left out of it too: its menu panel moves out to the window's left, but the 3D view beside it
# holds still between the camera's moves, so the check finds pixels fixed all along the way the panel goes (98% at
# its 4:3 place at 16:9, with no place out from it of its own) and reads it as a HUD left at 4:3. The replay, the
# credits' race and the track editor are left out of black at the sides (`unchecked black`): each has near-black with
# something drawn at its edge, which the check can't tell from black stopping short. The replay and the credits' race
# are races, so each window's is its own: the replay's stadium roof with a grey post at the 16:9 window's right edge
# as the camera pans (1.31 s in a row), the credits' dark forest at the view's side at 32:9 (0.11 s). The editor's
# track view is black round the arena, out to the panel at the window's left edge (at 32:9, 72 of 144 rows black to
# 109 pixels out from the view's side, held as long as the editor shows).
# Two steps go past what keys can do in a few seconds. A race's end: the computer drives the player's bike home over
# one lap, the word at 0x800E4848 written as 0x01000000 (gRiders[0].isCpu, which the game sets itself as a player
# finishes, and the three bytes after it cleared) and the event's laps, the high half of 0x800F0F68, as 1 beside its 6
# riders, both once the race is under way (the event's state at 0x800F0F48 is 5), since its start sets the laps; the
# finish then waits for A. And the credits, left by Start after 17 s.
